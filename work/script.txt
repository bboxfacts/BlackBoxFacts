Grey hair isn't grey. It's see-through.

Every hair starts out clear, like fishing line. The color gets injected from the root.

Deep in each follicle, pigment cells pump melanin into the hair while it grows.

When those cells quit, the hair grows in empty. Tiny air pockets scatter light, and clear looks white. Pull one out and hold it up to a lamp.

But here's the insane part. Stress really can turn you grey, and it isn't stress hormones.

In 2020, Harvard scientists stressed out mice with pain, confinement and chaos. Within weeks, their black fur grew in white.

The culprit? The fight or flight nerves wrapped around every single follicle.

Under stress, they flood it with noradrenaline. The pigment stem cells all panic, wake up at once, and burn through the entire reserve.

In those mice, the cells never came back.

And it gets weirder. The hair on your head is already dead, so it can't go white overnight.

But in 2021, Columbia researchers mapped single human hairs and found grey strands that went dark again, lining up with calmer stretches in people's lives.

Not everyone, not every hair. But your grey might be negotiable.

So next time you find one in the mirror, remember.
