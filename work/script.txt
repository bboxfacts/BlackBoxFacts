The moth in your closet never ate your sweater. Its babies did.

Adult clothes moths have shrunken mouthparts. They never eat a single thing.

They just mate, lay dozens of tiny eggs on your clothes, and die.

Those eggs hatch into little caterpillars, and they're the ones chewing the holes.

But here's the insane part. They're eating keratin.

That's the protein in wool, fur, feathers, and the hair on your head.

Keratin is locked together with sulfur bridges, so almost nothing on Earth can digest it.

These larvae can. Their guts are brutally alkaline, around pH ten, and loaded with chemistry that snaps those sulfur bridges.

Scientists still aren't totally sure how they pull it off.

And it gets worse. They love your sweat.

Sweat, skin flakes and food stains draw them in, so the dirty spots get eaten first.

That's why the holes hide in armpits, collars and folds.

Oh, and that moth fluttering around your lamp? Probably not one of them.

Clothes moths hate light. They hide in the dark, right where your wool lives.

So next time you blame the moth, remember:

Follow. Tomorrow's fact crawls out of somewhere worse.
