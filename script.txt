That tiny doorknob shock can hit thirty-five thousand volts.

And the doorknob didn't do it. You did.

Every step on carpet, your shoes rip electrons off the fibers.

Rubber soles are insulators, so that charge has nowhere to go.

Step after step, you turn into a walking battery.

But here's the insane part.

Your finger never even touches the metal.

That snap you feel? It's a tiny bolt of lightning between you and the knob.

Because voltage isn't what hurts. Current and energy are.

Your body holds so little charge that the whole zap is over in less than a millionth of a second.

And it gets worse in winter.
