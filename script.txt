Fruit flies don't eat your fruit. They eat the fungus growing on it.

That fungus is yeast. Wild yeast lands on your banana and starts fermenting the sugar.

And here's the insane part. The yeast is calling the flies on purpose.

It pumps out a fruity smell, chemicals called acetate esters, made by one gene.

In 2014, Belgian scientists deleted that gene.

The yeast went scentless, and the flies basically ignored it.

Why would yeast want flies? Because yeast can't walk.

The flies eat it, get covered in it, and carry it to the next fruit. A free ride.

In their tests, smelly yeast got spread around four times more than the scentless kind.

But it gets worse. The flies don't just snack.

A female lays around five hundred eggs, right on that fermenting food.

And her maggots eat the yeast too. In one study, yeast alone could raise them. Yeast-free grapes couldn't.

Egg to adult in as little as eight days.

That's why apple cider vinegar makes such a good trap. It smells like yeast at work.
