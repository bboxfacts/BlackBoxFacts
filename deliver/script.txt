Your pizza dough might be softened with boiled duck feathers.

And sometimes? Human hair.

The ingredient is called L-cysteine. It's an amino acid, and bakers add it as a dough conditioner.

Here's why. Gluten is a net of proteins tied together by tiny sulfur bridges.

More bridges, tougher dough. It fights back, snaps back, and takes forever to mix.

Cysteine snips those sulfur bridges, so the dough relaxes and stretches.

But it gets weirder. Your hair is held together by the exact same sulfur bridges.

That's why hair and feathers are packed with cysteine, and why they're a cheap place to get it.

Factories boil them in strong acid, and pull the cysteine back out.

A vat of feathers goes in. A white powder for your dough comes out.

Here's the insane part. A hair perm breaks those same sulfur bridges so your hair can be reshaped.

Pizza dough and a perm. Same chemistry.

Your baker basically gave your crust a tiny perm.

Today plenty of cysteine is brewed by microbes instead, and labels rarely say which kind you got.
