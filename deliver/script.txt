The corn in your poop is basically empty. You already digested the inside.

Everyone thinks corn just slides through you untouched. Wrong.

Every kernel wears a tough little jacket called the pericarp.

It's made mostly of cellulose, the same stuff as wood and paper.

And humans don't make cellulase, the enzyme that cuts it. So the jacket survives.

But here's the insane part.

Your teeth crack those jackets open, and your stomach acid and enzymes pour in and drain out the soft, starchy middle.

What comes out the other end is the empty skin, still shaped like a kernel, like a shed snakeskin.

Chew slower and you'll see way less of it. Gross? Yes. Useful? Also yes.

And it gets weirder. Scientists use this.

It's called the sweetcorn test. Eat a handful, start a timer, and wait for the kernels to reappear.

About a day is typical. Under twelve hours is fast. Two days or more is slow.

One study fed eight hundred sixty-three people bright blue muffins and timed their blue poop.

How fast they pooped was linked to which bacteria lived inside them.

So next time a kernel stares back at you from the bowl, just remember:
