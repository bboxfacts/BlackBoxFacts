Lime juice plus sunlight can literally burn blisters into your skin.

Doctors call it margarita burn, and it is very real.

You squeeze a lime at the beach, a little juice drips down your hand, and you forget about it.

A day later, that exact drip shape turns red and blisters.

Here's the insane part. It's not the acid.

Limes are loaded with chemicals called furocoumarins, the plant's own defense weapons.

On your skin, in the dark, they're harmless. They just sit there.

But when ultraviolet light hits them, they wake up and jam themselves into your DNA.

They staple the two strands of the double helix together, so the cell can't copy itself.

Your skin cells detect the damage and self destruct. That's the blister.

But it gets worse. Weeks later the spot turns dark brown, and it can stay for months.

Shaped like drips. Or fingerprints. Or an entire handprint.

Celery, parsnips, fig leaves and giant hogweed pull the exact same trick.

And the wildest part? Dermatologists use this same chemistry on purpose, with UV lamps, to treat psoriasis.
